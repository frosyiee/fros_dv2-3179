Malaysians' Everyday Consumer Life

The Cost Of Everyday Life Across Malaysia

Groceries, transport, rent and phone bills do not cost the same everywhere. This page compares how prices, incomes and household spending differ across Malaysia's 16 states and federal territories, and asks who is feeling the squeeze.

The Bigger Picture

First, the basics: where people live, what households earn and spend, and how prices have moved. Two terms come up often. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) tracks the price of a typical basket of everyday goods and services. Inflation is how much those prices have risen over a period.

Price rises hurt more when incomes cannot keep up. So how do prices and incomes line up?

The Biggest Pressure Points

Two things decide how much a price rise hurts: how fast prices climb, and how much a household earns.

What this means

Prices are up in every state since 2019, by 8% in Labuan and 21% in Putrajaya, and eating out, personal care and food rose fastest. Food has climbed much faster than housing costs almost everywhere. The squeeze is not only a big-city story: Kedah and Pahang pair lower incomes with faster price rises, and households in 12 of 16 states spent a larger share of their income in 2024 than in 2022. Kedah, Negeri Sembilan and Pahang combine a high share of income spent with fast price rises.

Everyday services follow a similar pattern. Water use per person varies by a factor of three between states, and rural areas still trail cities on internet access and computer use, although almost everyone with a connection uses a phone. Online shopping follows income to a degree, but Johor shows that a higher income does not guarantee more online buying. Men and women use the internet for shopping and banking at almost the same rate, while the over-60s and teenagers do less of it.

Keep the limits in mind. The online shopping survey covers one year, the income data is for 2024 and the prices run to August 2026. CPI shows how fast prices changed, not which state is more expensive. Online buying is a share of internet users, and survey figures for small states such as Labuan and Putrajaya are less certain. Water use per person assumes the figures are million litres per day, and Selangor's figure is shared with Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya.
